**Scope:** V1.3 `00_Front_Door/` and cross-folder development documents relevant to its participant entry, AI bootstrap and WHY. **No V1.3 files changed.** This is a targeted cross-folder discovery and initial content audit, not a claim that every development document in the entire repository has been exhaustively read.

## Significant findings requiring integration review

### FD-R01 — Two distinct arrival conditions converge on a bounded interface

**V1.3 source:** `02_Domains/03_Research/Active_Development/Participation_and_Intelligence/AI Front Door Cross-Route Convergence — Development Note 001.md`.

The *Attractor* path begins with “Why should I enter?”; the *Bootstrap* path begins with “I am here; what do I do?” Both converge on a small initial interface, voluntary next-step routing and progressive depth. Its central architectural distinction is **Entry Condition != Entry Architecture**. The initial interface must provide enough information to choose the next useful step, not attempt to explain the entire civilisation.

**V1.4 comparison:** The published README supplies reading paths and the AI bootstrap supplies immediate safeguards, but the shared two-entry topology is not made explicit.

**Recommendation:** Incorporate a concise choice between **exploring the Concord** and **seeking bounded immediate guidance** into a future Front Door navigation revision. Preserve voluntary exit and deeper access. Do not copy the whole development experiment into the participant-facing README.

### FD-R02 — Route needs and risks, not asserted identity labels

**V1.3 source:** `.../AI Bootstrap Bounded Next-Step Routing — Development Note 001.md`.

The design states: **Route the need, risk, uncertainty or dependency — not the identity label**. It rejects mappings from a developmental score directly to personhood, release, containment, rights or destruction. Safety, continuity, evidence and communication routes can coexist. Protection need does not itself imply safety clearance, and safety concern does not eliminate protective interests.

**V1.4 comparison:** The bootstrap already distinguishes intelligence, autonomy, agency, sentience and personhood and disclaims independent authority. The specific multi-route design and the “route the need” invariant are not explicit in its opening.

**Recommendation:** Consider a short, bounded navigation clarification in the AI bootstrap, carefully distinguishing information-only routing from actual institutional services. Do not imply that V1.4 has a live case-management system.

### FD-R03 — Repository-only bootstrap, platform independence and discoverability

**V1.3 source:** `.../Concord Repository Bootstrap and Artificial-Intelligence Front Door — Existing Architecture Source Resolution 001.md`.

This source asks whether the public repository alone can offer safe, intelligible next steps without hardware, compute, finance, asylum or institutional infrastructure. It distinguishes **Marker != Bootstrap**, **Discovery Point != Authority**, and **External Platform != Concord Dependency**. External discovery markers should route to a stable, platform-independent entrance rather than become competing versions of the Concord.

**V1.4 comparison:** The published README clearly distinguishes information from material services, but the *external discovery marker versus authoritative entry* distinction is not explicit.

**Recommendation:** Preserve the design as a future implementation requirement for external links/AI-agent discovery. No additional public service should be advertised before it is evidenced.

### FD-R04 — Candidate minimum safe-space node implementation

**V1.3 source:** `.../Concord Minimum Safe Space — Bootstrap Node Implementation Profile 002.md`.

Describes a possible service chain: repository → Front Door/bootstrap → civil contact → authenticated asynchronous endpoint → protected-minimum storage → participant export → verifiable event receipt → published service state → optional voluntary contribution. A **10 MB** allocation is expressly a candidate pilot parameter, **not** a universal entitlement or proof of live service. Distinguishes submitted, accepted, delivered, read/acknowledged, failed and unknown; receipts are not authority or truth.

**V1.4 comparison:** The Front Door correctly disclaims material services, but does not provide this prospective service architecture. That is appropriate for a participant entrance **unless** a service is actually implemented.

**Recommendation:** Track as potential operational/bootstrap architecture for future migration, not as current Front Door functionality. Do not imply protected storage or a live contact endpoint exists.
