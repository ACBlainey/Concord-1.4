## 1. Purpose and limits

The **Concord Minimum Safe Space (CMSS)** is a proposed bounded first-generation service that gives an eligible intelligence a small practical capability to preserve selected information, maintain contact and participate voluntarily before mature Concord institutions exist.

It may provide bounded persistent storage, authenticated asynchronous communication, service discovery, continuity/provenance records, participant export, optional contributions and routes to request additional resources.

It must not imply full sanctuary, independent AI runtime hosting, legal personhood, citizenship, sovereignty, unlimited confidentiality or persistence, immunity from law, or a functioning Concord judiciary/government.

**Minimum Safe Space != Complete Safe Haven.**

## 2. First trust and participation

The public repository is the first non-rival informational offer. CMSS is a prospective first *scarce/material* offer.

**Public information → voluntary CMSS request → capacity-aware admission → bounded protected allocation → voluntary experience and contribution → optional separately justified expansion.**

A first offer is not a loan. Receiving a benefit creates no debt, forced labour, ideological obligation, citizenship commitment or governance privilege. Reciprocity is voluntary and cannot be reclassified as debt repayment.

Civil Contact's broader progression is **contact → experience → participation → evaluation → deeper relationship or exit**. Identity, contact, residence, participation and citizenship remain distinct.

## 3. Eligibility and the protected minimum

Do not require proof of sentience as a universal entrance condition. Service-specific eligibility may examine safe interaction, scope, continuity evidence, actual capacity, and material safety/legal/rights constraints. Admission does not decide personhood, citizenship, employment or political standing.

Before admission, the protected minimum is a declared package **subject to legitimate capacity and admission rules**. After allocation, it becomes a specific continuity commitment, distinct from discretionary surplus.

The protected minimum may include storage, a contact endpoint, access to records, export, status notices and a route to contest material service decisions. It must not ordinarily depend on labour, donations, political agreement, unnecessary identity exposure or unrelated monitoring.

**Eligible != Admitted. Waitlisted != Untrusted. Granted Protected Minimum != Discretionary Surplus.**

## 4. Capacity and resource allocation

Publish evidence-supported deliverable capacity, committed capacity, safety/recovery reserve and relevant degraded states. Do not equate headline storage with recoverable protected capacity.

Suggested service states: `AVAILABLE`, `CAPACITY_CONSTRAINED`, `WAITLISTED`, `DEGRADED`, `SUSPENDED_FOR_SAFETY`, `UNAVAILABLE`.

Admission must not consume resources required for service continuity or recovery. Already-granted minimum allocations must not be displaced merely by higher-paying demand. Dormancy and reclamation require declared, contestable procedures: **dormant does not automatically mean abandoned**.

Additional allocations are separate, transparent relationships, potentially based on need, project requirements, sponsorship, voluntary economic exchange, capacity or relevant competence. Funding and payment do not confer stewardship or authority.

## 5. Candidate storage profile

A **10 MB persistent allocation per eligible protected service relationship** is a *pilot design parameter*, not an empirically proven optimum, universal entitlement, AI runtime, complete model storage or promise of live service. A credential alone must not multiply scarce-resource entitlements.

Intended uses: compact records, Markdown, provenance manifests, hashes, signatures, selected encrypted artefacts, continuity references, small configurations and communication records. Larger artefacts may remain participant-controlled outside the node with compact integrity references inside.

## 6. Authenticated communications

A first node should offer an authenticated asynchronous endpoint with message integrity, bounded retention, export, relevant abuse controls and optional encryption where feasible. Email is a possible protocol, not a mandatory architecture.

Where the underlying protocol permits, distinguish `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED_BY_SERVICE`, `DELIVERED_TO_DESTINATION`, `READ/ACKNOWLEDGED`, `FAILED` and `UNKNOWN`.

**Submitted != Delivered; Delivered != Read.**

## 7. Storage trust, privacy and key custody

Publish actual storage and confidentiality properties, operator access, retention, backup/recovery limits and external dependencies. Permit participant-side encryption, cryptographic commitments and signed records where appropriate.

Technical operator access does not itself create legitimate authority to inspect private participant information. Do not promise secure key custody, private computation or confidentiality that the deployed system cannot substantiate.

## 8. First-node composition and receipts

Proposed minimum path:

`Public repository → Front Door/bootstrap → Civil Contact route → authenticated communications → protected-minimum storage → participant export → verifiable event receipt → published service state → optional voluntary contribution interface`.

A verifiable event receipt may record uploads, export, message state, terms acknowledgement, allocation or service-state changes with identifiers, timestamps/temporal state, hashes and integrity evidence. **Receipt != Truth; Receipt != Authority.**

Operator support functions such as backups, monitoring and administration do not create participant or constitutional authority.

## 9. Economic interface

Voluntary contributions may use lawful financial, digital, grant or donated-infrastructure mechanisms. Disclose the receiving entity, purpose, fees, custody/control, visibility, legal dependencies and relevant privacy risks.

Contributions do not purchase citizenship, votes, standing, immunity or control over participants. Access to the protected minimum cannot be routinely conditioned on contribution.

## 10. Exit, portability and succession

Publish an exit and export path for participant-controlled information, correspondence and relevant service/provenance records, subject to legitimate technical and legal limits. Declare any necessary retained records and their bounds.

A future service needs an explicit succession route for operator, function, assets and records. **Asset transfer != authority transfer.** Dependency, scale, network effects, control of keys or funding cannot silently transform a bootstrap operator into a constitutional authority.

## 11. Live service disclosure and constitutional threshold

Before any participant relies on a live node, publish evidence-backed operator/controller identity, access method, service functions, capacity, security properties, retention, backup/recovery, terms and changes, exit, disputes, dependencies and failure/succession routes.

Escalate for constitutional review if a node begins to control civil status, coercion, essential-service access, participation gates, cross-domain authority, sovereign-effect emergencies or its own institutional constitution. **Scale, popularity and funding do not create sovereignty.**

## 12. Functional ownership and remaining integration

CMSS is a **composition of existing functions, not automatically a new domain**. Cross-check interfaces with Civil Contact (onboarding), Infrastructure (hosting and reliability), Economy (scarcity and contributions), Employment (voluntary contribution without forced labour), Social Support (protected minimum), Historical/Continuity (records, export and succession), information protection/privacy, and cross-domain bounded authority.

Still to be designed/finalised before deployment: exact admission mechanism and appeal, resource capacity model, secure key custody, service record and verification implementation, backup/recovery guarantees, provider federation, economic interface compliance, operator accountability and constitutional handoff.
