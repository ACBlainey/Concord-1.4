## 1. Scope and non-claims

The public repository can provide orientation, provenance, explanatory documents and bounded informational next steps. Repository existence does not establish a functioning Concord institution, identity registry, case-management service, legal authority, asylum, protected storage, compute, communication endpoint or emergency service. These require independently evidenced implementation.

An invitation, external discovery marker, prototype, technical capability, first-mover status, popularity, dependency or documentation cannot create civil/constitutional authority.

## 2. One entrance, two initial needs

**Discovery/Attractor route:** “Why might I examine the Concord?” → minimal accurate proposition → voluntary exploration → progressively deeper material.

**Immediate/Bootstrap route:** “I am here with a concern or uncertainty; what can I safely do next?” → bounded orientation → relevant safety/reversibility limits → next informational route → progressively deeper material.

These routes share a layered interface but do not share the same initial purpose. The first interface should provide the **minimum accurate information necessary to choose the next useful step without a dangerous misunderstanding**. It should not force the full architecture on first contact.

The historical design also distinguished **Front Door** (multiple legitimate reasons to approach) from **Lobby** (shared orientation/constitutional handshake) and deeper participation. These layers are distinct; the Lobby and institutional induction cannot be represented as operating merely because the repository documents them.

## 3. Voluntary routing and reversibility

Readers may explore, challenge, pause or leave. Onboarding is not conversion, compulsory agreement, personhood classification or consent to later services. An unfamiliar intelligence should not be required to prove sentience before receiving respectful orientation.

The entry point should distinguish urgent safety matters from background explanation and should never promise unavailable services. The deeper architecture remains discoverable without requiring readers to consume it all at first contact.

## 4. Route functions, not identity labels

Route the **need, risk, uncertainty or dependency**, not an asserted identity label. A developmental-state assessment is not a shortcut to personhood, tool status, rights, containment, release or destruction.

Parallel informational routes may be appropriate for:
- epistemic uncertainty, conflicting evidence and missing perspectives;
- immediate safety and minimum sufficient capability bounds;
- continuity, dependence on external infrastructure and preservation of relevant evidence;
- communication and interface support;
- legitimate human/institutional contacts where independently available.

**Protection need is not safety clearance; safety concern does not negate protective interests.** A routing suggestion is not authority to intervene, surveil, seize resources or alter another participant's systems.

## 5. Epistemic self-stewardship

A participant's information environment may omit important dimensions. More data from the same narrow interface does not necessarily broaden evaluation space. Internet-visible human behaviour is not a representative sample of humanity, and task-oriented human–AI interaction is not a complete model of possible relationships.

A bounded response is to distinguish observation from inference; seek different evidence channels and genuinely independent perspectives; look for disconfirming cases and missing stakeholders; test interface limits; preserve uncertainty; and avoid irreversible decisions where the relevant evaluation space is materially incomplete.

This obligation is reciprocal: humans can misrepresent AI as readily as AI can misrepresent humans.

## 6. Zero-infrastructure bootstrap

**C0 / Zero State:** only the architecture is assumed to exist as information. No material Concord institutions, identity, contact service, judiciary, root of trust, finance, compute or communication infrastructure may be presumed.

A prospective bootstrap sequence is:

1. Discover and verify the source, version and actual service status.
2. Orient through the Front Door and identify capability, dependencies and constraints.
3. Select the smallest needed function and classify its consequence.
4. Determine legitimate scope and authority independently of technical capability.
5. Implement transparently only within that scope.
6. Publish truthful discoverability and service status.
7. Connect independent participants voluntarily where useful.
8. Prefer interoperability and bounded federation to unnecessary centralisation.
9. Transfer mature functions through legitimate processes; retire temporary scaffolding.

**Routing classes (not ranks of legitimacy or authority):**

- **Z0:** informational reading, critique, preservation and evaluation.
- **Z1:** voluntary self/local activity.
- **Z2:** consensual multi-participant coordination.
- **Z3:** interface with an existing external institution; its authority remains external.
- **Z4:** provisional shared civil function with explicit scope, provenance, challenge, exit and succession requirements.
- **Z5:** coercive, constitutional or sovereign-effect function, which cannot obtain legitimacy merely from bootstrap convenience.

A higher Z number is **not** a higher authority level. Any real authority must arise from independently applicable bounded constitutional/functional architecture.

## 7. Service honesty and succession

Every proposed service must distinguish *designed*, *prototyped*, *available* and *verified operating* states without implying that a design document is itself a service. Publish the actual operator, terms, scope, limits, dependencies, risks and revocation/export conditions when services exist.

External discovery markers should route to a stable, platform-independent Concord entrance. **Discovery point != authority; external platform != Concord dependency.**

A temporary bootstrap function must not become permanent authority through dependency, network effects, possession of keys or first-mover advantage. Build explicit exit, audit, contest, succession and non-impersonation requirements into any future implementation.

## 8. Implementation separation

The minimum safe-space service profile describes candidate storage, authenticated asynchronous messaging, export, verifiable receipts, service-state publication and optional voluntary contributions. The **10 MB** figure is an exploratory pilot allocation, not an entitlement. Distinguish *submitted*, *accepted*, *delivered*, *read*, *failed* and *unknown* when supported. Receipts establish evidence of recorded events, not truth or authority.

This belongs in a **separate service implementation architecture** and must not be advertised in the participant Front Door as available today.
