# 161. Experimental Development Path

A possible experimental sequence is:

```text
Stage 1
Simulation

Stage 2
Small non-monetary controlled trial

Stage 3
Repeated trial with adversarial participants

Stage 4
Multi-community trial

Stage 5
Limited tokenised economic simulation

Stage 6
Sandbox monetary pilot

Stage 7
Independent replication

Stage 8
Larger operational trial
```

Progression should depend upon evidence.

Failure at one stage should generate revision rather than automatic escalation.

---

### 161.1 Stage Gates and Failure Outcomes

Each experimental stage must define the mechanism under test, predicted result, comparison baseline, material failure conditions, evidence capture, participant protections and competent go/no-go decision before testing begins. Failure, inconclusive results and unavailable evidence are distinct outcomes. Advancement requires evidence relevant to the next stage, not merely completion of the previous trial. Non-monetary coordination tests cannot by themselves validate monetary issuance or treasury solvency.

# 162. Adversarial Testing

Ratchet should be tested by participants explicitly trying to break it.

Adversarial tests should examine:

- fake contribution;
- collusion;
- identity multiplication;
- reputation manipulation;
- verifier capture;
- treasury gaming;
- parameter exploitation;
- AI optimisation;
- privacy attacks;
- governance capture.

A system tested only by cooperative participants has not been adequately tested for hostile environments.

---

# 163. Comparison Testing

Where possible, Ratchet mechanisms should be compared against alternatives.

For example:

- contextual reputation vs conventional rating systems;
- proportional evidence vs fixed evidence requirements;
- distributed verification vs central verification;
- Ratchet community contribution vs conventional grant allocation.

Without comparison, improvement may be difficult to establish.

---

### 163.1 Comparative Evaluation and Stopping Rules

Comparisons should predefine relevant outcomes, costs, failure rates, distributional effects, privacy burdens and second-order effects. A mechanism that improves one metric while worsening constitutional standing, accessibility or capture risk cannot be declared superior on the single favourable measure. Test reports must distinguish observed results from extrapolation, and retain unsuccessful or inconclusive findings. Repeated failure or material harm triggers redesign or suspension rather than automatic continuation.

# 164. Replication

A result obtained once by Ratchet's developers is not sufficient evidence of general reliability.

Independent replication should be sought.

Replication may include:

- different participants;
- different communities;
- different researchers;
- different software implementations;
- different cultures.

This is particularly important because the Concord has largely developed internally.

---

# 165. Evaluation Evidence

Assessments of Ratchet mechanisms must distinguish conceptual analysis, simulation, controlled trials, independent replication and observed outcomes. Evidence for one claim must not be represented as evidence for a different claim.

### 165.1 Evaluation-Space Completeness

Before accepting an assessment, examine plausible alternatives, untested populations, common-cause failures, adverse outcomes and material unanswered questions. This completeness check prevents premature closure without requiring every uncertainty to be eliminated before bounded action.

# 170. Open Questions

Ratchet retains substantial unresolved questions.

These include, but are not limited to:

### Contribution

- How should indirect contribution be recognised?
- How should long-term contribution be valued?
- How should harmful externalities offset apparent contribution?
- How should invisible or difficult-to-measure contribution be protected from neglect?

### Evidence

- What evidence is sufficient in different domains?
- How should verification independence be measured?
- How should AI-generated evidence be authenticated?
- How should evidence requirements scale without producing bureaucracy?

### Reputation and Trust

- How should contextual trust be represented?
- How should reputation move between systems?
- How should historical behaviour be weighted?
- How should competing reputation authorities interact?
- How should rehabilitation be demonstrated?

### Identity

- How should Sybil resistance operate without destroying privacy?
- How should AI copies and forks be treated?
- How should pseudonymous participation work?
- How should compromised identity be recovered?

### Privacy

- Which privacy-preserving technologies are suitable?
- Where are zero-knowledge techniques useful?
- What information must remain auditable?
- What information should eventually be deleted?

### Economics

- Can Progressive Monetary Backing remain stable?
- How should PBB and PBR be calculated?
- What backing assets or activities should qualify?
- How should monetary supply respond to changing conditions?
- Can Give Back operate without undesirable status effects?
- What baseline provision mechanism is appropriate?
- How should retirement and reduced capability be supported?

### Governance

- How should Ratchet governance be selected?
- How should capture be detected?
- How should parameter changes be authorised?
- How should local implementations interact with higher constitutional authority?

### AI

- What participation should non-sentient AI receive?
- What changes if an AI is recognised as sentient?
- How should AI multiplicity affect reputation?
- How should human–AI collaborative contribution be attributed?
- How can automated verification remain meaningfully independent?

### Scale

- Which mechanisms fail as participant numbers increase?
- Which functions should remain local?
- Which require common standards?
- How can interoperability occur without forced uniformity?

These questions are part of Ratchet, not embarrassments to be hidden.

The architecture should make its uncertainty visible.

# 172. Coordination Principles

The following are more mature conceptual principles within Ratchet:

- contribution broader than employment;
- effort is not automatically value;
- capability-aware participation;
- evidence proportionality;
- provenance;
- contextual trust;
- recoverable trust;
- reciprocal participation;
- reversible participation;
- anti-social-credit safeguards;
- bounded authority;
- controlled persistence;
- failure-driven learning.

These should still remain open to criticism and revision.

Operational architecture remains open to correction.

---

# 176. Final Architectural Boundary

Ratchet should coordinate.

It should not become the universal evaluator of civilisation.

It must not become:

- a universal human-value score;
- a permanent reputation state;
- a substitute constitution;
- an autonomous judiciary;
- an unlimited treasury authority;
- a mandatory surveillance architecture;
- an unreviewable algorithmic government.

The system's power must remain bounded by the wider Concord.

---

# 177. Final Architectural Principle

Ratchet can be reduced to the following core proposition:

> **Enable participants to make useful contributions, establish sufficient evidence of those contributions, develop contextual and recoverable trust, exchange capabilities and resources reciprocally, measure the resulting outcomes, preserve demonstrated improvements, correct demonstrated failures, and retain enough knowledge to learn from both.**

Everything else in Ratchet is an attempt to make that proposition operational without allowing its mechanisms to become new sources of uncontrolled power.

---

# 178. Conclusion

Ratchet began as a proposal for a new economic system.

Its development exposed a deeper dependency.

Before a contribution-backed economy can operate, the system must answer:

- what contribution means;
- how contribution is evidenced;
- how evidence is attributed;
- how evidence becomes trust;
- how trust remains contextual;
- how different participants cooperate;
- how reciprocal benefit is established;
- how failure is corrected;
- how improvement persists;
- how the mechanisms themselves remain constrained.

The resulting architecture is therefore broader than currency.

Ratchet provides a general protocol for:

> **participation, contribution, provenance, evidence, contextual trust, reciprocity, exchange, feedback and controlled persistence.**

Its monetary system remains an important application.

Specific mechanisms require the testing, safeguards and further design identified in their respective chapters.
